## 3. Body, part one

```
The Producer Price Index (PPI) measures the prices U.S. businesses receive for what they sell. Its headline measure, final demand, covers goods, services and construction sold to final buyers: households, businesses buying equipment, governments and buyers overseas.

Our new tool, the PPI Scuba Diver, breaks that headline into every item the Bureau of Labor Statistics weights in it, about 820 in a typical month, and shows how much each one added to or took away from the 12-month change. It covers every month from October 2016 to September 2026, and it is free to use.

And it has sharks.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
The diver sits as deep as the 12-month change in PPI final demand. Deeper means more inflation. Items that added to the change hang below as anchors, pulling the diver down. Items that took away from it float above, lifting the diver up. The size of each bubble is how much the item moved the total, and its color is how much its own price changed.

Press Play and the diver moves through 10 years. It sinks to 11.7% in March 2022 and rises near the surface, to -1.5%, in April 2020. Point at any bubble to see the item behind it, or switch to the table to see all of them. 

We also show our work. For every month, the chart says whether our items add up to BLS's published figure, and if they do not, why. In September 2026, PPI final demand was 6.3% higher than a year earlier, the largest 12-month increase since December 2022. One item added more than any other: gasoline, with a price 66.1% higher than a year earlier. It was one of 16 items whose prices rose more than 50% over the year. Tomorrow we will take a deeper dive into the September 2026 data.

Which items weighed the diver down in the month you remember? Dive in and find out.
```
